For my my final project I wanted to explore timbre interpolation as we were shown in class a video of this concept, allowing multiple different instruments to sound truly "blended together." So the implementation that was suggested to me and that I used was to first begin with an offline approach to first find two basic instruments that are playing the same note at approximately the same volume in order to analyze the underlying harmonic content of each using Fast Fourier Transformations(FFTs). I decided to start with trumpet and violin just because they sound quite different and found recordings of each of these instruments playing C5, cut each down to 2 seconds in the middle portion of the recording to account for both attack and decay, and loaded these wav files into Audacity where I then viewed the frequency analysis with the plot spectrum. For each of these spectrums, you see different spikes at relatively even spaced frequencies, each of which are harmonic partials, which give a particular instrument its character of special/unique way of sounding. I hovered over each spike and recorded the first 8 frequencies and amplitudes, giving me a table of 8 harmonics per instrument. It was interesting in simply how different each instrument's partials were, despite only taking measurements from 2 second clips. After gathering all of the harmonics of the trumpet and violin, I programmed, with the help of Claude, to mix the harmonics and perform additive synthesis to achieve the "blended" sound. To mix the harmonics, I had to first convert each of the amplitudes from dB to a linear scale to interpolate correctly, using the formula: 10^(dB/20). Once this was converted, each pair of harmonics were blended individually using linear interpolation, which is then fed into additive synthesis, assigining each of the amplitudes to a sinewave oscillator at their respective harmonic frequencies, and the sum of those waves is the new interpolated sound! After making sure this worked for the first two instruments, I decided to add a third, a flute. I was thinking of adding more instruments, but I had some difficulty finding steady, sustained state notes from a wider variety of instruments. The same process was applied to this instrument as well. I also wanted to add a few more features such as changing the note or octave, as well as visually showing the newly interpolated harmonics of the blended sound below the triangular pad, which Claude helped to design. This was definitely a super interesting subject to explore, as we didn't talk about it too much during class, I think the video that was shown was maybe during the physical modeling or FFT presentation? But that machine also employed a lot more instruments and much more unique sound as well such as the sitar. I would love to expand this further with more time and potentially find more samples from a wider range of instruments and techniques of playing to see how timbre interpolation could differ in each state. I am also curious about how many different instruments could be interpolated before they all begin to sound more or less like the same thing(in a 2D pad).
